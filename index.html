Map2Route: Benchmarking Compositional Language-Grounded Route Planning over Semantic Maps

Map2Route benchmarks whether an agent can resolve compositional references and generate complete routes that obey ordered requirements, scoped constraints, and graded preferences over semantic maps.

Anonymous Authors

Read the paper View code

Benchmark

Compositional language-grounded and constraint-aware route planning over semantic maps.

Each episode provides a pre-built semantic map, an initial pose, and an instruction. Models must resolve relational, comparative, and nested references, then generate a complete route that satisfies ordered must-pass and must-avoid requirements together with spatially or temporally scoped preferences.

1

Hard route requirements

Destinations are not coordinates. Agents must resolve relational, comparative, and nested references, then satisfy ordered must-pass and must-avoid regions.

2

Scoped soft preferences

Route quality depends on more than success. Preferences can apply globally, inside a spatial region, or during a particular route stage.

3

Complete-route efficiency

Path efficiency measures how directly the complete route fulfills all ordered requirements, rather than scoring only the final destination.

Grounding2Route

Grounding2Route separates compositional semantic grounding from geometric planning. The LLM does not output a trajectory directly; it produces a verifiable Route Semantic Intermediate Representation (RouteIR) that a deterministic planner compiles into a complete route.

1

Executable code-as-grounding

Compose read-only semantic-map APIs to resolve entities, relations, regions, and scopes.

2

RouteIR verification & repair

Turn provisional grounding into a typed, grounded specification and repair invalid references.

3

Scope-aware planning

Compile the verified RouteIR into a complete route with deterministic sequential search.

Results

Strong gains—and substantial headroom.

Hard Constraint Score (HCS) measures how many must-pass regions are reached in the correct order while respecting must-avoid constraints. Grounding2Route improves hard satisfaction, every soft-preference metric, and path efficiency.

Hard constraints 0.667 / 0.375 HCS

Grounding2Route improves Easy/Hard HCS by 0.299/0.251 over the strongest adapted baseline, showing substantially better compositional grounding.

Soft preferences Best of seven baselines

Grounding2Route leads every baseline on Near, Far, Relative, Path-Shape, and Clearance, indicating stronger route-level preference satisfaction.

Human-level gap Grounding2Route 0.375 vs. Human Demo 1.00 HCS

Despite these gains, Grounding2Route remains far from human performance on Hard episodes: it reaches 0.375 HCS and 0.08 Path-Shape, compared with 1.00 HCS and 0.99 for human demonstrations.

Ablation study

Controlled variants isolate the contribution of Grounding2Route’s grounding strategy, intermediate representation, verification-guided repair, and scope-aware preference modeling.

01

Executable grounding composes better

Code-as-Grounding reaches 0.579 HCS and 0.46 SPL, ahead of ToolCall, Direct ID, and schema-constrained prediction.

02

RouteIR fits heterogeneous route semantics

Replacing RouteIR with LTL reduces HCS/SPL from 0.579/0.46 to 0.439/0.36.

03

Verification-guided repair matters

Increasing repair rounds from zero to three raises HCS from 0.395 to 0.579 and Relative from 0.56 to 1.11.

04

Preferences need explicit scopes

Globalizing temporal or spatial scopes leaves HCS nearly unchanged but weakens Far and Relative; removing soft costs degrades every soft metric.

Difficulty analysis

Complexity compounds.

Map2Route isolates three complementary pressure points: deeper compositional references, more candidate instances before disambiguation, and longer chains of hard constraints. Grounding2Route remains strongest, but both methods degrade steadily as each factor grows.

Real-world experiments

Routes that leave the benchmark.

We evaluate Grounding2Route on physical robots across 48 episodes in eight indoor scenes, testing whether compositional instructions, hard constraints, and scoped preferences transfer beyond the benchmark.

72.22%Real-world HCS

Of the lost HCS, 73.75% comes from semantic-map construction errors, 22.5% from grounding or route planning, and 3.75% from SLAM issues.
