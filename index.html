RouteSemBench: Benchmarking Compositional Language-Grounded Route Planning over Semantic Maps

RouteSemBench benchmarks whether an agent can resolve compositional references and generate complete routes that obey ordered requirements, scoped constraints, and graded preferences over semantic maps.

Benchmark

Compositional language-grounded and constraint-aware route planning over semantic maps.

Each episode provides a pre-built semantic map, an initial pose, and a instruction. Models must resolve relational, comparative, and nested references, then generate a complete route that satisfies ordered must-pass and must-avoid requirements together with spatially or temporally scoped preferences.

1

Hard route requirements

Destinations are not coordinates. Agents must resolve relational, comparative, and nested references, then satisfy ordered must-pass and must-avoid regions.

2

Scoped soft preferences

Route quality depends on more than success. Preferences can apply globally, inside a spatial region, or during a particular route stage.

GroundPlan

GroundPlan separates compositional semantic grounding from geometric planning. The LLM never outputs a trajectory directly; it produces a verifiable route specification that a deterministic planner compiles into motion.

1

Executable code-as-grounding

Compose read-only semantic-map APIs to resolve entities, relations, regions, and scopes.

2

RouteIR verification & repair

Turn provisional grounding into a typed, grounded specification and repair invalid references.

3

Scope-aware planning

Compile the verified RouteIR into a complete route with deterministic sequential search.

Results

Strong gains—and substantial headroom.

Hard Constraint Score (HCS) measures how many must-pass regions are reached in the correct order while respecting must-avoid constraints. GroundPlan improves across hard satisfaction, every soft-preference metric, and path efficiency.

Existing methods remain far below human performance when language specifies not only where to go, but how to get there.

Across seven representative adapted baselines, GroundPlan achieves the strongest overall results. Yet the remaining gap—especially on compositional Hard episodes and path-shape preferences—shows that RouteSemBench is far from saturated.

Difficulty

Complexity compounds.

RouteSemBench isolates three complementary pressure points: deeper compositional references, more candidate instances before disambiguation, and longer chains of hard constraints. GroundPlan remains strongest, but both methods degrade steadily as each factor grows.
